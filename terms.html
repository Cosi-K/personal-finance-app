PayStamp terms

How PayStamp works and what you can expect from us. In plain English.

Last updated 27 November 2026

The short version

  • PayStamp is completely free. There’s no paid plan, no trial and no card needed.
  • It’s a planning tool. It never holds or moves your money, and it isn’t financial advice.
  • Your plan is yours. You can delete your account and everything in it at any time.

Free, for everyone

Every part of PayStamp is free to use while we build it with our first users: the Monthly Planner with stamps and Tools, Investments, Savings, Debt, Records and Insights, the What if calculators, email reminders, the Couples planner and PayStamp Hustle. There are no adverts and we never sell your data.

If we ever add paid extras, we’ll tell you first, with at least 30 days’ notice. Planning your pay will always stay free, and nothing you’ve made will be taken away or locked.

Beta features

Parts marked Beta (the Couples planner and PayStamp Hustle) are new and still being improved. They may change as we learn what works. Please tell us if anything looks wrong.

Founding members

The first 100 people to stamp a month become founding members, with a founder number that’s theirs for good. Founding members may get extra perks as PayStamp grows.

Not financial advice

PayStamp is a planning and learning tool. It never holds or moves your money. Its tips, lessons and calculators are general guidance, not personal financial advice. For advice about your own situation, speak to a regulated adviser, or try the free, impartial MoneyHelper service.

Using PayStamp fairly

Please use PayStamp for your own planning, don’t try to break or overload it, and don’t share your account. We may suspend accounts that are being misused, and we’ll tell you why.

The law

These terms are governed by the law of England and Wales. If you live elsewhere in the UK, you can also use your local courts. If there’s a problem, please email us first and we’ll try to sort it out. Nothing here affects your legal rights as a consumer in the UK.

How we handle your data is explained on the privacy page.
