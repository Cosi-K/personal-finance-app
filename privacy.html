Privacy

What PayStamp stores about you, why, who helps us run it, and how to delete it all.

Last updated 27 November 2026

The short version

  • PayStamp stores your email address and the plan you type in. That’s what it needs to work.
  • It never connects to your bank and never asks for bank logins or card numbers. PayStamp is free, so it never takes payments.
  • There are no adverts and your data is never sold. PayStamp keeps a simple log of which pages and buttons are used (never amounts or anything in your plan) to see what helps people. You can switch it off.
  • Your plan is stored only so it’s there each time you sign in, and a copy is kept on your device so PayStamp works offline. Other users can never see it, and it isn’t viewed, shared or used for anything else.
  • You can download your plan or delete your account at any time from your badge › Your details.

Who we are

PayStamp is a monthly pay planner at paystamp.co.uk, run by an independent sole trader based in the United Kingdom, trading as PayStamp. For your data, PayStamp is the “controller” under UK data protection law (UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018).

Questions or requests: hello@paystamp.co.uk.

Who can see your plan

Your plan is kept in PayStamp’s secure database for one reason: so it’s saved between visits. It’s locked to your account, and the database only hands it to someone signed in as you, so other PayStamp users can never see it.

Access to the database is only used to keep PayStamp running. Plans aren’t read, analysed, shared or sold. The only time anyone would look at yours is if you ask for help with a problem in it, and then only with your permission.

Legal reasons

Our legal reasons (“lawful bases”) are: providing the service you signed up for (your account, plan, and the Couples planner), and our legitimate interest in improving PayStamp and keeping it secure (the usage log, feedback and technical details). We don’t use your data for anything else.

PayStamp doesn’t need sensitive details. Please don’t put things like full card numbers, account numbers or passwords into your plan or your links.

Some of these companies may handle data outside the UK. Where they do, they use the safeguards UK law requires, such as approved contract terms.

Cookies and storage on your device

PayStamp doesn’t use advertising or tracking cookies. Your browser keeps a sign-in token so you stay signed in, a copy of your plan so PayStamp works offline, and an invite code if you arrived from a friend’s link. PayStamp also keeps a copy of its own page and files so it opens quickly and without a signal. Signing out removes your plan from the device. Cloudflare may set a strictly necessary security cookie.

How long we keep it

We keep your account and plan for as long as you have an account. The usage log is deleted automatically after 13 months. When you delete your account, your plan, profile, sign-in and usage log are deleted straight away, and so is any Couples table you share. Suggestions are kept without your name or email, so they’re anonymous. Our providers may keep backups for a short time before they’re overwritten.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct, download or delete your data, or object to how we use it. Much of this you can do yourself:

  • Download your data: your badge › Your details › Download my data. Records can also be saved to Excel.
  • Switch off the usage log: your badge › Your details › Your data.
  • Stop sharing with a partner: More › Couples planner › Stop sharing. The shared table is deleted for both of you.
  • Delete your account: your badge › Your details › Delete my account.
  • Anything else: email hello@paystamp.co.uk. We’ll reply within a month.

If you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) at ico.org.uk. We’d appreciate the chance to put it right first.

PayStamp isn’t financial advice

PayStamp is a planning tool. Its tips, lessons, calculators are general guidance to help you think about your money, not personal financial advice. For advice about your own situation, speak to a regulated adviser, or try the free, impartial MoneyHelper service.
